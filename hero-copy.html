The hero line: what it says vs. what it earns

Right now the biggest text on the site is a tagline, not a description. That costs you twice — visitors don't immediately know what you sell, and Google has no idea what to rank you for.

The problem, measured

Your <h1> and <title> are the two most heavily weighted pieces of text on any page. Here's what's currently in them:

The headline finding: your location doesn't appear in your H1 or your title tag. For a business that only serves one metro area, that's the most valuable missing piece. Nobody searches "elevate your event" — they search "golf simulator rental raleigh."

The five-second test

Someone lands on the page and gives it about five seconds. "Elevate Your Event. One Swing at a Time." could plausibly be a caterer, a DJ, a photo booth, a party rental company, or a golf coach. The photo does the explaining that the words should be doing.

Why Option 3 wins

It stops treating "tagline" and "headline" as the same job. The H1 states plainly what the business is and where it operates — the part Google reads and a stranger needs. The tagline sits directly underneath in italic, still prominent, still yours. You lose nothing and gain the thing that was missing.

The general rule: a tagline is what you say once someone already knows who you are. A headline is what you say to someone who doesn't. A business with strong brand recognition can lead with a tagline. A local business people haven't heard of yet cannot.

Fix the title tag at the same time

Your current title prints the brand twice and never mentions where you operate:

Now:  919 Golf Co. | 919 Golf Co. – Mobile Golf Simulator. Book Today!
New:  Mobile Golf Simulator Rental | Raleigh & The Triangle, NC

Squarespace appends the site title automatically, so the SEO title only needs the descriptive half. Set it in Pages → Home → Settings → SEO.

Also worth doing: the homepage currently has two <h1> tags — "Elevate Your Event." and "One Swing at a Time." are each their own H1. A page should have exactly one. Combining them into a single heading fixes it without changing the design at all.
